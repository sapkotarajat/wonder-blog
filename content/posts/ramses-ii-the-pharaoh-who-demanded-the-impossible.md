# Ramses II: The Megalomaniacal Pharaoh Who Demanded Reality Itself

Picture this: It is 1274 BC. You are an Egyptian soldier standing on the blood-soaked plains of Kadesh, surrounded by tens of thousands of charging Hittite chariots. Your commanding officers have panicked, and your royal guard is on the verge of total annihilation. Through sheer tactical miscalculation, your supreme leader—**Pharaoh Ramses II**—has walked right into an enemy trap. 

Most rulers would cut their losses, retreat into the shadows, and quietly lick their wounds. But not Ramses the Great. 

When Ramses returned home to his capital of Pi-Ramesses, he didn’t admit defeat. Instead, he demanded that the entire empire celebrate his *glorious, solo victory* over forty thousand enemy troops. He forced temple scribes to chisel immense epic poems into sacred walls, claiming he stood entirely alone while crushing thousands of chariots with his bare hands. 

Welcome to the world of Ramses II, the ancient Egyptian monarch who didn't just want your taxes and your absolute loyalty—he demanded total control over **reality itself**. 

In this deep dive, we are pulling back the curtain on ancient Egypt's wildest royal edicts. From rewriting history to demanding impossible engineering feats, here is how Ramses the Great bent an entire empire to his unbreakable will.

---

## Rewriting History: The Ultimate PR Campaign

To understand the sheer scale of Ramses' demands, you have to look at how he weaponized propaganda. The Battle of Kadesh was, by all accounts from neutral modern historians, an embarrassing tactical stalemate. Hittite forces outsmarted the young pharaoh, nearly costing him his life and his army.

Did Ramses accept this? Absolutely not. 

*   **The Poem of Pentaur & The Bulletin:** Ramses issued royal decrees demanding two separate, highly embellished epic texts be engraved across at least five massive temple complexes. 
*   **Zero Tolerance for Editing:** Priests and stonecutters faced severe physical punishment if they altered even a single word glorifying his god-like physique. 
*   **Mandatory Public Recitation:** Visitors were required to read these accounts aloud upon entering sanctuaries. Ramses essentially forced everyday Egyptians to verbally recite state-sponsored fiction as sacred religious scripture.

*Actionable Takeaway:* If you ever need to pivot after a bad quarter at work, just channel your inner Ramses. Go big, commission a marble bust of yourself, and insist that your disastrous project launch was actually a "masterclass in solo leadership."

## Claiming the Past: The Art of Usurping Monuments

Ramses was never satisfied with just building his own new monuments. He made the astonishing demand to **claim the past**. 

He commanded royal stonemasons to systematically erase the cartouches (royal name rings) of prior pharaohs—including legendary rulers like Amenhotep III and Thutmose III—and carve his own name deeply into their ancient stone statues. 

To prevent any future monarch from doing the exact same thing to him, he ordered his cartouches to be cut **several inches deeper** into the granite than normal. Modern Egyptologists call this *usurping*, but to Ramses, it was a simple royal mandate: every glorious stone in Egypt had always belonged to him.

## Engineering Pi-Ramesses: A Metropolis Built on Sweat

When Ramses decided to move Egypt's administrative capital hundreds of miles north, he demanded a paradise rise from swampy terrain in record time. 

Founded around 1278 BC, **Pi-Ramesses** grew into a sprawling metropolis spanning over six square miles. How? Ramses simply demanded that **fifty thousand builders work year-round** without taking seasonal agricultural breaks. 

*   **Exotic Imports:** He required royal gardens to cultivate turquoise-colored faience tile canals and foreign flora imported from Syria and Nubia. 
*   **Arborist Nightmares:** If a tree species couldn't survive the brutal Nile Delta heat, royal arborists faced public demotion. This forced them to engineer complex, primitive shade canopies just to keep exotic shrubs alive.

---

## The Ultimate Family Corporation: 100+ Children

Family planning for Ramses was another theater of extreme royal demands. During his astonishing **sixty-six-year reign**, the pharaoh engineered a strategy to fully replace every civil administrator with blood relatives. 

Historical records show he sired over one hundred biological children—historians confirm at least forty-eight to fifty sons and over forty daughters. 

*   Every child received vast personal estates, private guards, and specific administrative quotas they had to report directly to him. 
*   His demand for biological dominance turned the entire upper echelon of Egyptian governance into a strictly controlled, high-stress family corporation.

## Breaking Artistic Tradition for Queen Nefertari

His royal edicts regarding his favorite queen, **Nefertari**, shattered centuries of Egyptian artistic convention. 

At the smaller temple of Abu Simbel, dedicated to the goddess Hathor, Ramses demanded that sculptors carve statues of Nefertari at the **exact same physical height** as his own figures. 

For over a millennium, tradition mandated that royal consorts reach only the height of the pharaoh's knees or calves on monument facades. Defying the deeply superstitious priestly caste, Ramses threatened artistic overseers with exile if Queen Nefertari’s thirty-five-foot colossi were carved even an inch shorter than his own colossal portraits.

## Living Deities and Sunlit Alignments

Ramses didn't wait until he died to become a god. Beginning around his thirtieth year of rule, during his first *Sed festival*, he demanded ritual recognition as a physical deity walking the earth. 

Priests had to burn incense before gigantic statues of the living pharaoh christened with titles like **'Ramses the God'**. Ordinary citizens were required to leave daily bread offerings before these stone effigies, praying to the stone ruler for rain, Nile floods, and healing from deadly scorpion venom.

### The Architectural Marvel of Abu Simbel
Consider the staggering logistics of the great temple at Abu Simbel, carved directly out of a solid sandstone cliff face in Nubia. Ramses demanded that his royal engineers align the entire mountain sanctuary so sunlight illuminated his inner sanctuary **only twice a year**. 

*   **The Magic Dates:** On October 22nd and February 22nd, the morning sun traveled two hundred feet inside the mountain to illuminate his divine face alongside the gods Ra and Amun. 
*   **The Exception:** Ptah, the creator god of the underworld, was commanded to remain perpetually shrouded in darkness.

## Maintaining the Illusion: Red Hair and 1,000-Ton Stones

Maintaining a divine physical appearance was an absolute requirement that plagued his royal attendants for decades. 

*   **The Red Henna Secret:** Archaeological analysis of his mummified corpse revealed that Ramses was naturally a pale-skinned man with flaming red hair. Because red hair was intensely associated with the chaotic, violent god Set, Ramses demanded **daily applications of imported red henna leaf pastes** even as he approached his nineties.
*   **The Thousand-Ton Statues:** The pharaoh's demands for quarry production exhausted the sandstone beds of Gebel el-Silsila and the granite mines of Aswan. Scribes recorded that Ramses demanded individual monolithic statues weighing over **one thousand tons** to be extracted without fracturing. Transporting a single sixty-foot colossus required twelve hundred men rowing giant wooden barges down the flooding Nile, facing immediate execution if the stone cracked.

## Diplomacy Through Intimidation

Even Egyptian diplomacy was transformed into a showcase of relentless financial intimidation. 

When negotiating boundary treaties with neighboring kingdoms, Ramses demanded royal tribute specifically paid in **lapis lazuli and silver**—metals not naturally occurring in Egypt. 

If a local vassal prince arrived short by even a few pounds of raw lapis lazuli, Ramses demanded the vassal surrender sons and royal heirs as captive collateral to serve inside Egyptian military academies.

## Surveillance, Lions, and Fusing Spines

Ramses' life was defined by control down to the smallest detail:

1.  **The Surveillance State:** At Deir el-Medina, the walled village where artisans lived, scribes documented every single worker absence on limestone fragments called ostraca. Workmen were penalized if they missed carving shifts due to scorpion bites, home beer brewing, or eye infections.
2.  **Royal Pets:** Ramses ordered royal scouts to round up wild Libyan lions, keeping several fully grown, tamed beasts as personal bodyguards. His favorite lion, 'Slayer of His Enemies', slept at the foot of his royal dais.
3.  **The Aging Body:** As Ramses suffered from severe *ankylosing spondylitis*—an inflammatory disease that completely fused his spine into a permanent hunch—he refused to let his subjects see him bent like an ordinary mortal. He demanded that sculptors portray him as eternally youthful and muscular, even when he was an eighty-five-year-old walking with wooden canes.

## The Final Triumph: A Passport for a Dead King

Ramses II held the throne for sixty-six years and two months, living to roughly age ninety. Because he outlived twelve of his intended crown princes, royal succession became a chaotic nightmare of his own making, ultimately passing to his thirteenth son, Merneptah.

Even his final resting place in tomb KV7 was engineered with tyrannical specificity, finished decades before his actual physical death. 

Yet, his arrogance had ironic modern consequences. In 1974, French Egyptologists noticed dangerous fungal growth consuming his mummy, necessitating emergency preservation in Paris. Because French law requires **every person entering the country to possess a valid passport**, the Egyptian government complied. 

They issued the three-thousand-year-old dead pharaoh an official modern passport:
*   **Nationality:** Egyptian
*   **Date of Birth:** 1303 BC
*   **Occupation:** *King (deceased)*

When the mummy landed at Le Bourget Airport, the French Republic received Ramses II with full military honors usually reserved for living heads of state, complete with jet fighter escorts and brass bands.

## Conclusion: The Legacy of Ramses the Great

History is written by the victors, but few victors manipulated history quite like Ramses II. By demanding absolute perfection, rewriting military defeats, and bending art, architecture, and international diplomacy to his whims, Ramses ensured that he would never be forgotten.

Whether you view him as a tyrannical megalomaniac or an extraordinarily effective master of personal branding, one thing is certain: **Ramses II successfully forced the world to remember him exactly the way he demanded.** 

*Next time you feel like your boss is making unreasonable demands, just remember—you aren't being asked to source a thousand tons of granite, dye your hair with red henna at age ninety, or secure a modern passport three millennia after your death. Keep building your empire!*
